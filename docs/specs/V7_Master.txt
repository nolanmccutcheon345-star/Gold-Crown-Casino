V7 critical technical patch: exactly-once bot decisions and replay-safe
money
1. Decision execution contract
A bot DecideRequest includes immutable handId, seatId, stateRevision, street, actorToken, policyVersion,
observationHash and decisionId. Its stochastic random draw is isolated from deck random generation. Store
(decisionId, observationHash, sampledAction, sampledSize, policyVersion, botRngStateOrDrawToken)
once. UI rerender/pause/reload does not resample. On reducer submission validate state revision, turn, legal
amount, current policy and one-time decision token. Late/duplicate worker returns are ignored and logged; no
wallet-changing action is triggered by animation or rejected computations. Maintain invariant: once decisionId
is committed, exactly one authoritative action event exists, or it is explicitly canceled with no financial effect.
Document choice for crash between sampling and commit and test it.
2. Snapshot, event and privacy consistency
Production randomness is cryptographic and not identical to deterministic test streams. A private resume snapshot
can store unrevealed cards and internal random state only in private device storage for the single-player local-
mode threat model. Redacted hand exports may include exactly observed showdowns but not mucked holes or

future deck. Replay from sealed snapshots matches canonical event graph and ledger; a public export must never
be accepted as a trusted active-session save without validation. If storage unavailable/quota exceeded: show a clear
error, pause safely, offer export/backup; never silently create a new virtual bankroll with earlier profits overwritten.
3. Seat and room changes
Freeze cash RuleProfile version, rake, chip denominator, blinds, straddles and buy-in eligibility at hand start;
changes apply after resolution. Cash-eligible seated players can include sit-outs, but rake caps use dealt-in count
when that is the selected room rule. Check cash-time waiting blind / straddle / dead button under chosen room.
Tournament event owns different blind/ante posting rules, not a generic cash policy.
4. Opponent memory and realism
Only actual revealed showdown cards (subject to chosen room policy), public betting sizes/actions and permissible
notes may update a bot’s opponent memory. Private folded holes never leak through study stats or cross-bot
references. Sample individual trait vectors once at seating; allow bounded adaptive belief updates from observations
rather than random personality flips. Generate action AND bet size jointly, with normalized legal distribution; use
current price, multiway action behind, range arrived on this street, draw quality and effective stacks.
5. Fixed-context evaluation with uncertainty
Rather than demand 100k simulations per rare category, freeze a context-stratified test set and compute action+size
frequencies on available decision opportunities. Report N, Wilson/bootstrap intervals as appropriate, missing
strata, pre-registered direction of changes, and sample eﬀiciency. Do not mark human-calibrated when human
sample absent. Separate player-population matching, exploitability/strength, and Hero’s reported simulated profits.
Increase runs where necessary until precision requirement met or mark INSUFFICIENT_DATA.
6. Tournament event timing and concurrency
Event time increments by explicit deterministic policy; each hand locks blind-level snapshot at hand start (TDA
2026 Rule 25, room variants separately). All background tables run exact rules, no invented knockouts; pause must
not mutate field. H4H carries cycle identity, table completion barriers, and simultaneous-finish ranking policy. Web
worker queues are cancelable, memory bounded and cannot mutate finalized ledger on late reply. Load-test 18/45/90
player seeded fields on explicit device/browser targets. Policy version changes to bot strength in mid-tournament
apply at a documented boundary (usually new hand), not retroactively.
7. Explicit exclusions
No live-platform botting, datamining or covert assistance. No jackpot drops disguised as generic rake, no long-lived
opponent clairvoyance, no pretend solver-certification, no EV-based percentile guarantee. No RIT/bomb/other
poker variant hidden behind flags before their own settlement oracle exists.

B. Human realism’s measurement problem

cash vs stage, player image. Stratify by suﬀicient opportunities; absent cells are INSUFFICIENT_DATA, not 0%.

style, rather than reassigning labels per hand.

D. Metric definitions
• cash_profit = sum(settled per-hand Hero net across selected sessions) excluding all deposits,
wallet manual changes and in-progress pots; bb/100 = 100 * sum(per-hand net / hand-specific BB)
/ eligible hands when mixed stakes are included (declare denominator and allow per-stake views). If all
hands are a fixed BB this equals 100*(net_total/BB)/hands.
• App-clock $/hour uses actual tracked playing duration and explicit pause handling. A hypothetical live-paced
rate may be displayed only with the assumed hands/hour and warning, never as observed earnings.
• Tournament ROI = (prizes received - sum buy-ins including fees and bounties) / sum buy-ins
including fees and bounties, or display a separately labeled alternative cashflow basis; track each ticket,
entry and re-entry explicitly. Tournament chip count is not cash profit.
• Confidence intervals: block by sessions/tables where independence assumptions fail and expose effective
sample size; avoid exact certainty from a few hundred hands.
E. Performance targets (initial, confirm on devices)
On a defined modern phone and desktop, target p95 input-to-render latency under 250ms for local cash actions
(excluding animation) and no unrecoverable stall in a 1,000-hand loop; report long tasks, memory growth, and
total field run duration for 18/45/90-player MTTs. Targets are proposed engineering goals, not externally sourced
industry standards; measure and revise with evidence.

Exact objective
A bot is believable when its observed play over many hands resembles a consistent human of its defined style,
including mistakes, variable but plausible sizes, responses to history, and strategic constraints. A bot is strong
when it performs well against independent opponent strategies. Those properties are correlated at times but never
interchangeable. Neither implies accurate representation of actual Tulsa or high-stakes populations absent data.
Persona vector instead of label factory
Each seat’s stable latent object includes: preflop position/action-range priors; passive/active axis; risk tolerance
by street and stack; value threshold; bluff/semi-bluff mixture; multiway caution; sizing elasticity and anchoring;
draw-chasing tendencies conditional on pot odds/implied odds; trap propensity; river polarization; opponent-image
beliefs; response to short stack; tournament ICM/risk premium; bounded observation memory; within-session noise.
Initialize one reproducible persona vector at seat assignment from declared archetype distribution. Variation is
correlated (e.g., loose station usually calls more than it raises); perturbation does not erase archetype identity or
randomly punt stacks. The ‘hundreds of personalities’ roadmap is a combinatorial sampling and validation problem,
not 100 hardcoded nominal labels.

legally visible actions and Hero-made notes; hidden persona and unavailable stats do not appear, even if host

Causal tests per policy change
For an altered draw-call curve, compare matched opportunity sets with same hole cards, board, stack, action
and legal sizes. Measure observed probability shifts, not demand every individual decision change. For a nit vs

contexts, mark it not tested. Verify action-size normalization and no illegal sampled amount. Long session hidden
identity must remain stable, while play variability remains credible.

Human-play research priorities
1. Obtain legal,
consented multi-session hand histories or observed live records with button/position,
stakes/rake, board, preflop and postflop sizes, effective stacks and opponents’ showdown/muck visibility.
Separate disclosed identities, no third-party unauthorized data collection.
2. Avoid claiming a real live $1/$2, $1/$3, $2/$5, $5/$10, $10/$20, $20/$40 or $50/$100 frequency from generic
strategy articles. A blind level alone does not imply skill. Sample player pools, table mix and locale matter;
low stakes often have a wide range of recreational and regular styles. Treat ‘loose multiway $1/$2’ as a
synthetic selectable scenario unless directly measured.
3. Build holdout by full players/sessions and, ideally, different rooms; measure conditional log loss of both
action and size, river/multiway calibration, trajectories and expert-blind resemblance; publish uncertainty
and coverage. Do not silently generalize modern high stakes from historic online data.
4. Preserve public competitor design inspirations but do not copy protected art or assume their private poker
algorithms are known.
