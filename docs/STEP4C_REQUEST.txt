AI TEXAS HOLD EM — STEP 4C: INDEPENDENT BOT REALISM, EXPLOIT TESTING AND POPULATION VALIDATION

Continue development of the existing AI Texas Hold Em ChatGPT Site, currently published as Version 6.

Use the V7 Master Directive, 302 acceptance requirements, 86 deferred features, Acceleration Addendum and completed Steps 4A–4B as governing requirements.

Do not rebuild the application, alter Poker MaxEV, or weaken any existing test requirement.

OBJECTIVE: Independently evaluate and improve whether our eight bot archetypes behave like credible human No-Limit Texas Hold’em opponents across different stakes, seats, stacks, betting sequences and complete hands.

Do not merely increase simulation counts or adjust frequencies to satisfy predetermined test thresholds. Identify genuine weaknesses, trace their causes, correct them and demonstrate improvement on unseen scenarios.

1. INVESTIGATE THE TAG OPENING-RANGE DISCREPANCY

Step 4B reported a designed 100BB TAG first-in range of 9.48% UTG and 19.99% on the button.

Determine:

* Whether the scenarios were six-handed, nine-handed or ten-handed.
* Whether these represent all hands played, raises first in, or only selected 169 starting-hand classes.
* Whether percentages correctly weight the 1,326 possible starting-hand combinations.
* Whether early-position names were mapped correctly for each table size.
* Whether randomization, skill modifiers, population settings or rake affected the figures.
* Whether the displayed designed ranges agree with actual decisions produced by the bots.

Compare comparable six-max 100BB conditions with independently published preflop references, such as GTO Wizard-related resources, ART/GTO and other reputable training sources.

Treat solver-style strategies as technical reference baselines, not mandatory targets for recreational archetypes.

Correct erroneous or excessively restrictive TAG and strong-regular ranges where supported by the findings.

Repeat the measurement for 2, 6, 9 and 10 seated players and multiple stack depths.

Report before-and-after results, including definitions, sample sizes and exact test configurations.

2. BUILD AN INDEPENDENT BEHAVIORAL EVALUATION HARNESS

Separate the evaluation system from the bot policy implementation so the bots cannot merely pass tests by reproducing their own design assumptions.

Use controlled, reproducible random seeds and independent policy configurations.

Evaluate:

* All eight principal archetypes.
* Multiple individuals within the same archetype.
* Unknown and Custom configurations.
* Different skill levels.
* Low-, middle- and high-stakes population presets.
* Heads-up, six-handed, nine-handed and ten-handed games.
* Shallow, normal and deep effective stacks.
* Different rake and betting environments.

Measure actual completed-hand behavior, not merely isolated decision outputs.

Preserve test inputs, policy versions, outcome data and reproducible failure examples.

3. MEASURE REALISTIC POPULATION BEHAVIOR

Collect opportunity-based measurements for:

Preflop:

* VPIP and PFR.
* Limping and overlimping.
* Raise-first-in by position.
* Isolation raises.
* Cold calls.
* 3-bets, 4-bets and squeezes.
* Fold-to-3-bet.
* Blind defense.
* Open sizes.
* Heads-up and multiway participation.

Postflop:

* Flop continuation betting.
* Turn and river barreling.
* Check-raising.
* Fold-to-continuation-bet.
* Draw calling and raising.
* Value betting by hand class.
* Bluffing by street.
* River bluff-catching.
* WTSD, W$SD and WWSF where definitions permit.
* Bet-size distributions.
* Showdown and non-showdown outcomes.

Segment by archetype, position, stack depth, board type, previous action, heads-up/multiway status and stake environment.

Report exact opportunities and confidence intervals for key measurements. Treat repeated decisions from the same player or session as potentially correlated.

Avoid interpreting rare decisions using tiny samples.

Use sufficient targeted sampling to estimate important conditional frequencies with useful precision; report insufficient coverage explicitly instead of inventing thresholds or filling missing observations.

4. TEST FULL-HAND COHERENCE

Generate and inspect complete hand histories covering:

* Unopened and limped pots.
* Single-raised and 3-bet pots.
* Deep-stacked value hands.
* Multiway pots.
* Check-raises and delayed bets.
* Flush draws and straight draws.
* Turn and river runouts.
* Large river bets and overbets.
* Bluff-catching.
* Missed-draw bluffs.
* Slow-playing.
* Trapping.
* Short-stack all-ins and side pots.

Audit whether earlier actions produce plausible later-street ranges.

A calling station should retain a recognizable calling bias without being incapable of folding.

A nit should value premium hands and usually avoid excessive speculative aggression.

A maniac should take aggressive lines with strategic and psychological coherence, even when overbluffing.

A strong regular should demonstrate better conditional decisions, not simply tighter ranges or more frequent raises.

Record representative high-quality hands, questionable hands and clearly defective hands.

5. BUILD INDEPENDENT EXPLOIT-PROBE OPPONENTS

Create evaluation-only agents with strategies designed to expose specific policy errors.

Probe:

* Excessive folding versus small bets.
* Excessive calling versus oversized bets.
* Automatic river bluffing after missed draws.
* Never bluffing without strong draws.
* Predictable bet sizing associated with hand strength.
* Excessive passivity with premium hands.
* Inadequate response to changing effective stacks.
* Weak multiway decision-making.
* Incorrect river bluff-catching.
* Unrealistically broad continuation ranges.
* Artificially favorable treatment of Hero.

Use held-out seeds, novel boards, unseen stack configurations and opponents not used to tune the original policies.

Run mirrored seats and role-swapped evaluations where appropriate.

Distinguish desirable human-like exploitable mistakes from mechanical defects or accidentally exploitable algorithms.

Measure outcomes with uncertainty intervals. Do not claim mathematical exploitability bounds unless they have actually been established by a suitable solver or formal method.

6. TEST HIDDEN-INFORMATION INDEPENDENCE

Verify that changing Hero’s undisclosed cards while preserving everything legitimately visible to a bot does not change that bot’s decision distribution.

Likewise, changing future undealt cards must not alter current decisions.

Check public-action range models for:

* Correct card removal.
* Valid normalized combination weights.
* Legal use of only observable information.
* Proper response to action likelihoods.
* Sensible treatment of uncertain and previously unseen opponents.
* No accidental access to engine-internal cards, deal order or Hero outcomes.

Verify no hidden-information advantage is used to make bots artificially difficult or easy.

7. EXPERT AND HUMAN-PLAY COMPARISONS

Research reputable poker resources and publicly documented strategy frameworks relevant to:

* Live $1/$2 and $1/$3 recreational games.
* $2/$5 and $5/$10 cash games.
* Tougher online six-max and higher-stakes environments.
* Loose-passive multiway tables.
* Position- and stack-dependent strategies.
* Tournament transitions that will matter later.

Separate direct observations of real human decisions from coaching claims, solver examples and our synthetic assumptions.

If suitable legally usable human hand histories or decision datasets are available, apply an independent evaluation set with a held-out portion.

If unavailable, prepare an expert-review sample of complete hands and classify findings as analytically supported, expert judged, hypothetical or quantitatively measured.

Do not claim experts reviewed hands unless actual independent experts performed that review.

Do not fabricate field statistics, human calibration, solver outputs or opponent profitability.

8. IDENTIFY AND FIX THE HIGHEST-IMPACT DEFECTS

Prioritize changes that materially improve gameplay realism:

* Wrong action-frequency definitions.
* Incorrect position handling.
* Excessively tight or loose baseline ranges without a plausible profile explanation.
* Incoherent postflop sequences.
* Missing effective-stack effects.
* Overly predictable bet sizing.
* Implausible draws and bluff-catching.
* Faulty opponent-range updates.
* Bots exploiting inaccessible information.
* Bot behavior biased toward making Hero win.

Fix confirmed problems in the source code, add regression tests, and rerun independent scenarios.

Preserve intended personality differences and human mistakes.

Do not optimize every archetype into the same strong strategy.

9. VERIFY PERFORMANCE AND EXISTING FEATURES

Retain:

* One authoritative poker engine.
* Correct betting, side pots and payouts.
* Persistent bankroll and hand history.
* Correct statistical accounting.
* Guest access.
* Mobile 2–10-player perimeter seating.
* Session creation and safe exit.
* Recovery after reload.
* Bot decision idempotency.
* Historical policy-version support.

Test gameplay performance with 2, 6, 9 and 10 players, including on narrow mobile screens.

Measure bot decision latency, memory usage where available and browser responsiveness.

Prevent evaluation harnesses from interfering with live game performance.

Execute automated regression, independent PokerKit comparisons, accounting simulations, browser testing and relevant new behavioral tests.
