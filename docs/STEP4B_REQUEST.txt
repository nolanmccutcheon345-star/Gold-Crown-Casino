PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Improve the existing eight synthetic poker opponent archetypes so that their decisions become significantly more consistent with plausible real-human No-Limit Texas Hold’em play across low-, middle- and high-stakes cash games.

Focus on strategic reasoning, conditional action frequencies, hand selection, stack-depth sensitivity, opponent modeling and believable human mistakes.

Optimize for actual working gameplay and verified improvements, not additional documentation.

1. INVESTIGATE THE POCKET-ACES PROBLEM FIRST

Step 4A reported the following raise frequencies for AA in an unopened preflop scenario, with 1,000 opportunities per profile:

* Nit: 50.7%.
* Calling station: 27.1%.
* TAG: 96.2%.
* Strong regular: 96.3%.

These results raise serious concerns about realism.

Before modifying the policy:

1. Reproduce the exact scenarios.
2. Verify that they are truly unopened, first-in opportunities.
3. Record position, effective stack, blinds, prior actions and available legal actions.
4. Distinguish a limp, raise, all-in raise, check and fold.
5. Verify that the test harness classifies actions correctly.
6. Inspect the decision path and personality parameters producing the results.
7. Identify whether the issue is policy logic, test construction, action classification or some combination.
8. Correct the underlying problem instead of hardcoding a cosmetic frequency.

Premium hands should be treated coherently by each archetype. Occasional slow-playing may be appropriate for particular players and situations, but it must be deliberate and plausible.

Create regression tests for AA, KK, QQ, AK, weaker broadways, small pairs and suited connectors across positions.

2. POSITION-SPECIFIC PREFLOP RANGES

Implement or improve explicit, versioned starting-hand range models.

Account for:

* 2–10 players.
* UTG, middle position, cutoff, button, small blind and big blind.
* Unopened pots.
* Single limpers and multiple limpers.
* Open raises and cold calls.
* Isolation raises and squeezes.
* 3-bets and 4-bets.
* Facing different raise sizes.
* Short-handed and heads-up adjustments.
* Archetype, skill, population and player-specific tendencies.

Use actual hand-combination frequencies and valid conditional probabilities.

Ensure different actions produce coherent ranges. For example, a nit’s 3-bet range should generally be much tighter than a competent LAG’s, while a calling station should have a meaningfully different calling distribution.

Do not blindly apply solver ranges to recreational players.

3. STACK-DEPTH INTELLIGENCE

Improve decision-making at:

* 20bb.
* 40bb.
* 60bb.
* 100bb.
* 150bb.
* 200bb and deeper.

Account for effective stacks, stack-to-pot ratio, commitment thresholds, speculative hand value, implied odds, reverse implied odds and all-in incentives.

Deep-stacked and short-stacked versions of the same opponent should not behave identically.

Use legal stack-aware sizings and preserve existing engine validation.

4. PUBLIC-ACTION OPPONENT RANGE UPDATES

Create or strengthen a weighted opponent range-estimation system.

Initial opponent ranges should be conditioned on observed player tendencies, position, stack depth and prior public actions.

Update range weights after each observable action.

Use the updated distribution to inform:

* Value betting.
* Bluff selection.
* Bluff-catching.
* Draw decisions.
* Thin value.
* Continuation betting.
* Check-raising.
* Turn and river decisions.

The range model must never use an opponent’s undisclosed hole cards, future cards or information unavailable to the acting bot.

Do not mistake a bot’s true internal policy for information another player would automatically know.

Use probabilistic uncertainty and prevent the opponent model from becoming artificially omniscient.

5. ADVANCED POSTFLOP DECISION-MAKING

Improve decision policies for:

* Heads-up and multiway pots.
* Dry, wet, paired, monotone and connected boards.
* Static and dynamic board textures.
* Made hands, draws, redraws and blockers.
* Range and nut advantage.
* Equity realization.
* Pot odds and implied odds.
* Fold equity.
* Protection and value extraction.
* Continuation betting and delayed continuation betting.
* Turn barreling.
* River bluffing and bluff-catching.
* Check-raising.
* Slow-playing and trapping.
* Facing overbets, small bets and unusual sizing.
* River decisions after specific prior betting lines.

Evaluate future-street implications rather than treating every street as an unrelated isolated decision.

Avoid rigid rules such as always bluffing a missed draw or automatically betting every top pair.

6. REALISTIC HUMAN ERRORS

Each archetype should possess plausible weaknesses.

Examples:

* Calling stations overcall appropriate situations but still recognize sufficiently extreme threats.
* Nits overfold marginal spots and avoid risky bluffs, while strongly valuing premium hands.
* Maniacs overbluff and overaggress, but do not raise randomly regardless of hand and board.
* Recreational players may use inconsistent sizing, chase draws or become overly attached to pairs.
* TAGs have disciplined ranges and relatively coherent postflop decisions.
* LAGs pressure capped ranges and bluff selectively.
* Strong regulars demonstrate more accurate range awareness, bet sizing and stack-sensitive decisions.

Make these mistakes context-dependent, internally consistent and stable enough to be recognizable.

Preserve distinct individual personalities within each archetype.

9. ANTI-EXPLOIT TESTING

Create controlled evaluation opponents capable of probing weaknesses in the bot policies.

Test whether simple strategies can systematically exploit pathological behaviors, such as:

* Excessive folding to small bets.
* Automatically calling every draw.
* Excessive limping with premium hands.
* Never bluffing the river.
* Always bluffing missed draws.
* Betting the same percentage regardless of hand strength and range.
* Predictable sizing tells.
* Overcalling large bets with weak hands.
* Failing to respond to stack depth.
* Artificially soft play against Hero.

Report discovered weaknesses and improve them without erasing intended recreational-player mistakes.

Do not equate making every bot unexploitable with making every bot realistic.
